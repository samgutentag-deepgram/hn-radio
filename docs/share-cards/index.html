HN Radio share cards

What a developer's Flux TTS credits actually buy, as a picture that drops into a lifecycle email or a social post and points back at the episode. Marketing's ask was “as opposed to having a button or a link, something to show them.”

Use the toggle, top right. These are dark cards and most email clients are white, so flipping the page is the closest look at how one lands without sending yourself a test.

The set

Three real episodes at the ends and the middle of what the show actually costs, and two renders at forced rates. The rate is not confirmed, so the layout has to hold at $0.06 and at $0.20 as well as where the archive really sits, which is $0.10 to $0.27.

What is on every card

  1. What rendered it and what it is. The Deepgram / Flux TTS lockup and the show name.
  2. What this episode cost, in dollars, read from the episode's own cost block. Never a constant: the figure is computed from the exact text that was billed.
  3. What that rate means, as how many episodes the $200 signup credit covers.
  4. Where to go next, as visible text, because an image is not clickable in every client.

The two orbs are the episode's actual voices, in their real per-voice palettes. The co-host rotates, so the cast on the card changes every episode.

Notes for a designer

  • The type is not the brand face. This repo ships no font binaries, so the cards land on Arial locally and DejaVu in the container, the same way the site lands on system-ui. One line changes when a licensed file can live in the repo.
  • Every colour, size and spacing value is in one file, hn_radio/cards/tokens.py, and the colours in it are read out of web/brand.css rather than copied. A restyle is one file, and the cards cannot drift from the site they link to.
  • Static raster only. No web font, no JavaScript, no player, no iframe: an email client would strip all four. The alt text above each card carries the cost and the pitch for the readers whose client blocks images.
